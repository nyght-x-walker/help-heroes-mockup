Task 1 • Interpret + Abduct

Five quotation flowers

Quotation 1 • The Silent Trade-Off • Maria

"I've started screenshotting the slides and reading them like a textbook. It's not the same, I miss the explanations, but it's the only way I can keep up."

💡 Creative workaround, not real learning ⚖️ Degraded experience beats none 😞 Quiet shame, "not the same" ❓ Will the degree count the same? 🕐 Harder work, thinner results 🛣️ Surviving, not understanding
Inference: Maria trades depth for access on purpose. She wants the degree more than the system wants her. Shame plus overload ends in burnout or a quiet exit.

Quotation 2 • The Impossible Choice • Maria

"Professors say just watch the recording later. Downloading a 45-minute video uses half my data for the month."

⚠️ Staff assume access that isn't there ✖️ Study vs essentials, pick one 🚪 Built for someone else 🔇 She stopped explaining 😞 The "fix" makes it worse
Inference: Lectures compete with job applications and emergencies for the same megabytes. Silence after unheard explanations keeps the problem invisible and uncounted.

Quotation 3 • The Burden of Apology • James ⭐

"I'm always apologizing. To professors for late work, to group members for missed meetings, to family for the data."

🙈 Blames himself, not the setup 🏋️ Feels like an inconvenience 😢 Shame eats belonging ⛓️ Sorry for things outside his control 👥 Belonging slips away
Inference: Apology has become identity. Structural gaps read as personal failure, which silences him in class, in groups, and when help is near.

❀ Quotation 4 • The Double Bind • James ⭐ core insight

"I want to ask for help, but I don't even know what to ask. I don't know what I don't know."

🔒 Needs help, lacks the words to request it 📖 Hidden curriculum, never inherited ⏸️ Stuck between needing and asking ❓ Reads as disengaged, actually lost 🧰 Missing navigation skills
Inference: Asking itself demands vocabulary James was never given. Nobody hands him those words, so the confidence to self-advocate never forms.

Quotation 5 • The Invisible Student • Aisha

"Professors don't realize some of us work and study at once. They think we're just students with nothing else to do."

🫥 Her reality goes unrecognized 🎓 System built around a "typical" student ✊ Resentment builds ⚖️ Risks underperforming at both 💀 Strain tips toward dropout
Inference: The wound is recognition, not just workload. Two demanding worlds, support from neither, and disengagement follows when the institution won't bend.

Task 2 • Point of View

Three PoV statements

PoV 1 • Maria

I met Maria, who drives 20 minutes to library Wi-Fi and screenshots slides to save data.

I was inspired to discover she knows she learns less deeply and fears her degree counts less, yet persists because she values it more than the system values her.

It's all about helping Maria learn with confidence, without trading education against scarce resources.

CHOSEN ✓

PoV 2 • James

I met James, who apologizes for late work, missed meetings, and needing help, and believes he burdens the system.

I was inspired to discover he burns to ask for help but can't phrase the question. Institutional knowledge is a foreign language to him.

It's all about helping James navigate and belong, without apologizing for existing.

PoV 3 • Aisha

I met Aisha, fitting study into workday cracks, unseen by her professors.

I was inspired to discover she feels invisible to a community built for students with no other life.

It's all about helping Aisha learn on her terms, recognized as different from the "typical" student.

Choosing a direction • PoV 2, James

Belonging, not just connectivity

"Always apologizing" reaches past bandwidth into shame, invisibility, and feeling like a burden. The environment itself can un-belong a student. That opens a wider design space than tech fixes: community, expectations, and institutional knowledge made legible.

The problem to pursue

How might we help first-generation students navigate online learning without feeling like outsiders who constantly apologize for their circumstances?
