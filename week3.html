Deep dive • Chapter 3

When Connectivity Fails

Online learning went from optional to central. Campus labs used to be the backup; after COVID, hybrid stuck and reliable connectivity became the assumption, so every failure hits harder.

Known fixes: downloadable materials, recorded lectures, email submission. They help, yet downloads still cost data, recordings kill live questions, and email assumes she can get online at all. None touch the social cost of absence.

Open questions

  • • How to keep learning social without real-time presence?
  • • Design that routes around outages instead of coping?
  • • Which low-bandwidth tools are we overlooking?

😟 I worry about students going invisible • 🤔 I keep wondering what workarounds they invent next • 😤 It angers me that infrastructure rations education

Deep dive • Chapter 4

Beyond the Screen

University was always relationships and belonging, not just content. First-generation students started out behind socially; online doubled the isolation: no family guidance and no peer ties.

Known fixes: forums, virtual office hours, study groups. Forums feel performative, office hours need scheduling plus bandwidth, groups drop irregular attenders. None build belonging.

Open questions

  • • Genuine community on unstable connections?
  • • Which low-bandwidth social tools sit unused?
  • • Networks first-gen students can draw on for years?

😢 Losing the campus fabric hurts to watch • 💡 Low-bandwidth community still feels possible • 🤝 Above all, that loneliness is real

Five user types and their problems

🌾 The Rural Student

No broadband, lives on mobile data. Locked out of video, late submissions, talked over in discussions.

🦸 The Sharing Student

One device and data plan for the whole family. Fights family for bandwidth, studies at midnight, no privacy.

💼 The Working Student

Studies on work Wi-Fi in stolen moments. No desk, no routine, work and study colliding.

😟 The Anxious Student

Falling behind, invisible to staff. Won't ask for help, imposter syndrome, quietly disengages.

🔧 The Resourceful Student

Night downloads, library Wi-Fi, clever hacks. Still behind, burning out, solving it all alone.

Task 2 • Observe + Engage

Interview guide: 5 open questions

1. "Tell me about the last time internet problems blocked your classes or assignments."

Catches a concrete failure and the raw feeling behind it. Probes: what happened next, how did it feel.

2. "What are your biggest challenges as a first-gen student learning online?"

Surfaces life beyond bandwidth. Probes: what makes that hard, and can you give an example.

3. "Walk me through a typical day of coursework online."

Maps a full day to find the pain points. I ask where they study, which device, who shares the line.

4. "What would make online learning feel easier for you?"

Invites their fixes and exposes unmet needs. Probes: what would that look like, seen it anywhere.

5. "How do you stay connected to professors and peers despite all this?"

Charts the social layer and the workarounds already in use. I ask what works and what fails.

Three semi-structured interviews

🌾

Maria, 19

First year • rural, patchy broadband

signal 1/4

"I drive 20 minutes to the library for lecture videos. Last week it closed early mid-lecture. I nearly cried."

"Professors say watch the recording later. A 45-minute video eats half my monthly data."

"I screenshot slides and read them like a textbook. Not the same, but it's how I keep up."

"Will my degree mean the same thing when I graduate?"

"On Zoom without video, the group forgot I was there."

😤 Frustrated at the effort just to show up • 😢 Sad about slipping behind • 🤨 Anxious her degree counts less

🦸

James, 20

Second year • shares device + data with family

signal 2/4

"Five of us share one connection. I study after 11 pm when everyone's asleep, then I'm wrecked next day."

"I missed deadlines to timeouts. I keep screenshots of the errors to prove it wasn't me."

"Three platforms, each breaking differently on slow internet. A guessing game, daily."

"I'm always apologizing. To professors, to group members, to family for the data."

"I want to ask for help, but I don't even know what to ask. I don't know what I don't know."

😰 Exhausted from negotiating • 😞 Embarrassed, always sorry • 🤯 Overwhelmed by platforms • 😟 Isolated, unsure how to ask

💼

Aisha, 22

Third year • part-time job, studies on work Wi-Fi

signal 3/4, no time

"I download what I can at lunch and catch up after work. By then I'm exhausted."

"I join lectures on my phone on break. Can't participate, scared the manager walks in."

"Office hours are while I'm working. One email took three days. Assignment already due."

"The class WhatsApp is my lifeline, but by night everything's decided."

"Professors think we're just students with nothing else to do."

😩 Burned out juggling both • 😤 Resentful at being unseen • 💪 Determined anyway

Patterns

  • • Connectivity is one layer; social and logistical weight piles on
  • • Workarounds are clever, draining, and expire fast
  • • Official fixes misread the constraints
  • • Students feel unseen by staff
  • • Peer ties fray first

Surprises

  • • Screenshots as a lecture substitute
  • • Group projects hit hardest
  • • Students don't know what support exists, or how to ask

Open questions

  • • Learning that bends to constraints, not constant hacks?
  • • Feeling seen without more screen time?
  • • Video-free formats that teach just as well?
