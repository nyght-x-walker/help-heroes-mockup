02 • The Design Challenge

THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

Redesign the online learning experience for first-generation university students in a world in which access to high-speed internet is unreliable.

Complexity

  • • Multiple interconnected factors (tech, family, finances, support)
  • • Multiple stakeholders with conflicting needs
  • • Symptom of deeper problems (inequality, infrastructure)

Uncertainty

  • • No single, definitive problem formulation
  • • Multiple valid explanations
  • • Unknown unknowns & unintended consequences

03 • Understanding the user

UNDERSTANDING THE USER: JAMES

I met James, a first-generation student who has learned to apologize, for late work, for missed meetings, for needing help, and who has internalized the belief that he is a burden to the system.

I was inspired to discover that he desperately wants to ask for help but does not even know what to ask, because the institutional knowledge that some of his peers inherited from their families is a foreign language to him.

It's all about helping James navigate and belong without feeling like an outsider who has to apologize for existing.

🧑🏽‍🎓

James, the Sharing Student

Key insight

“I want to ask for help, but I don't even know what to ask. I don't know what I don't know.”

Key findings

  • ✅ The ask button landed: “That's me!”
  • ✅ Badges felt like pride: “I'd show these off”
  • ✅ The Wall normalized asking, others had the same questions
  • ✅ Text-only sipped data: “Uses almost no data”

What users said

  • “It says ‘Good evening, James.’ That's nice — personal.”
  • “I would actually press this. It doesn't feel scary.”
  • “A reward for being brave enough to ask.”

09 • Key takeaways

KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. Wicked → clumsy solutions

No perfect answer. Messy institutions, multiple strategies.

2. Empathy is essential

James's insight unlocked everything. Users reveal unspoken needs.

3. Help-seeking is strength

Reframe shame as courage. Community over solo struggle.

4. Iteration → innovation

Build → Test → Learn → Improve. Testing showed the way.

5. Design thinking is leadership

Enable collaboration • make sense of ambiguity • encourage constructive conflict.

“It's not about finding the perfect answer. It's about making genuine progress and learning from what works and what doesn't.”
