Assignment 1 Wicked problem s-01141

Accepting the Challenge

Why this brief refuses to behave: everything connects, nobody agrees on the problem, and there is no finish line. Rittel and Webber territory.

Redesign the online learning experience for first-generation university students in a world in which access to high-speed internet is unreliable.

Complexity + uncertainty The iceberg Risks + moves

Complex

One tug moves everything

Tech, course design, family, work, money, support: pull one thread and the rest shift. Make recordings downloadable and you can fill a cheap phone or strand the student who needed the live room.

Uncertain

One brief, three problems

Is it the connection, the course design, or the missing institutional knowledge? Each answer builds a different solution. Formulating the problem is the problem, and unknown unknowns ship with it.

"The formulation of a wicked problem is the problem."

Rittel and Webber. I can't fully grasp the brief until I've sketched a solution.

"What satisfies one group may be abhorrent to another."

Students, staff, admins, IT, families: five definitions of the same mess. Below.

The iceberg

The visible struggle sits on deep water.

Same mess, five definitions

Frame it as infrastructure, design, or equity, and the solution changes completely.

Students: the problem is flexibility. Learning should bend.
Professors: the problem is engagement. The room should answer back.
Admins: the problem is outcomes. The numbers should move.
IT: the problem is security. The systems should hold.
Families: the problem is clarity. It should be usable by all of us.

Expected trouble, planned moves

🎯

Who exactly am I designing for? First-generation students aren't one group. → Interviews and observation before assumptions.

⚔️

Stakeholder fights. Staff resist, admins count costs. → Involve early, prototype something touchable.

🧪

Side effects, guaranteed. → Build small, test fast, treat failures as tuition.

⚖️

No right to be wrong. Consequences land on real students. → Stay humble, state limits, keep the design adaptable.

🏁

No finish line. → Milestones now; better responses over final solutions.

Bottom line

Nobody solves this in any final sense. That is what makes it wicked. Empathy, collaboration, iteration, and experiment can still move it. The goal isn't perfection. It's something that genuinely helps first-generation students learn online, plus an honest record of what worked and what didn't.
